What the petals are

In Japanese, ば is just は with two short strokes added at its shoulder. Those strokes are what turn the sound ha into ba — so they are literally the two marks that make irobako out of iroha. Replacing them with two petals means the flower is doing the work of the accent, not sitting next to it as decoration.

Neither colour is new: the blue is the goi flower (#7c83ff), the pink is the kanji flower (#ff8fbb). The Latin name splits at the same point — iro blue, bako pink.

Worth checking on your screen: I positioned the petals against the shoulder of the は by measurement, but I have no browser here to see it in Shippori Mincho — I checked it in a different Japanese font. If they sit too high, too low, or crowd the こ, tell me which and it is a one-line nudge.
