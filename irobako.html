いろばこ

Japanese only — no Latin under it. ば is は with two short marks at its shoulder, the dakuten, and those two marks are the petals: one the blue of the goi flower, one the pink of the kanji flower.

on paper

on goi’s black

on the kanji site’s pink

vertical, and shrunk down

Notes

The petals do the work. Those two marks are exactly what turns iroha into irobako, so the flower is the accent rather than an ornament placed beside it. In the vertical setting they sit at the upper right of the は — which is where dakuten belong in vertical Japanese too, so the same idea holds in both directions.

Why not kanji. 色箱 would also read irobako and looks more formal, but kanji has no は for the marks to sit on, so the whole petal idea disappears. Kana keeps it.

Worth checking on your screen: I placed the petals by measuring against a real ば, but there is no browser here, so I checked it in a different Japanese font rather than Shippori Mincho. If they sit too high, too low, or crowd the こ, say which and it is a one-line nudge.
